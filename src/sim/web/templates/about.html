How it works

A student project about backend engineering. The finance is simple on purpose.

What it does

You give it an amount, a risk level and a time span. It starts from a list of large US companies, keeps the industries you chose, drops companies with negative earnings or extreme debt, ranks the rest and keeps a handful. Most of the money goes into broad funds, the rest into those picks. Then it replays history day by day and compares the result with simply buying the S&P 500.

The three portfolios on the dashboard are a forward test: they were fixed on their start date and are only valued since. They cannot be tuned afterwards to look good.

What it cannot tell you

  • It only knows today's companies. Firms that went bankrupt are missing, so past results look better than they would have been.
  • The range of outcomes is not a forecast. It reshuffles past returns. It cannot show a crisis that is not in the data.
  • Trading costs and taxes are ignored.
  • The rules are a simple baseline, not a way to beat the market.

Under the hood

A Python backend (FastAPI), a job queue in Postgres and a worker. The database is limited to 2 GB of memory on purpose. Pressing "Run simulation" only orders the work; a worker does it in the background and the page shows the result when it is ready. The server has no open ports: this site reaches you through an outbound tunnel.

Source code, design notes and measured benchmarks are on GitHub.

Privacy

No accounts, no cookies for visitors, no tracking, no third-party scripts or fonts. To limit abuse, a scrambled form of your IP address is kept in memory for up to a day. It is never stored in a database or a log.
